## Preface — Why & How This Book Was Made

Before Chapter 0, a short and honest account of why this book exists and how it came to be — because in this book's case the two answers are unusual, and you deserve both of them plainly.

### Why This Book Exists

This book is an on-ramp. It assumes you have never studied electronics, never touched a radio, and are not entirely sure what a ham is — and it walks you from exactly there to a passed Technician exam. The method is teach first, align to the exam second: every chapter explains the underlying idea in plain language, and only then shows you the pool questions that idea answers. You are not memorizing a list; you are learning just enough radio for the list to make sense.

Everything here is aligned to the NCVEC 2026–2030 Technician question pool, the public document from which every exam question is drawn, valid for exams given from July 1, 2026 through June 30, 2030. Because the pool is public and fixed for those four years, this book can print all 409 questions verbatim — and does, in Appendix A, each with its answer key and a plain-language explanation of why.

It is also the first book of a three-book series. This book, *Your First Ham License*, is the Technician course. After it come *Your Next Ham License*, the General course, and *Your Last Ham License*, the Extra course — the full ladder from curious to fully privileged.

### How It Was Made

This book was built by a multi-agent AI workflow, and we would rather tell you that plainly than hide it. Here is what that actually means.

The official NCVEC pool — public domain, like FCC Part 97 — was ingested verbatim and double-parsed from two independent source formats, cross-checked to zero disagreement. An accuracy canon was written first and treated as law: every exam fact pinned and sourced before a single chapter existed. Parallel writer agents then drafted the chapters and figures against that canon, and span-auditor agents re-verified every factual claim against the canon and every quotation against the pool.

The gate the book had to pass was mechanical, not a matter of taste:

- An eight-check automated audit, including verbatim-pool fidelity: all 409 questions in Appendix A compared byte-exact against the structured pool, every answer key matched.

- Forty original figures, hand-authored as themeable SVG or plotted directly from the math — including the three NCVEC pool diagrams, which were redrawn as original SVGs, never copied.

- An eight-voice audiobook edition, synthesized from the same text you are reading.

- Fifty-two tooling tests keeping the build, the audit, and the exam generator honest.

The integrity rules were simple: original prose throughout; Part 97 and the question pools are public domain and are used as such; and no fabricated quotations, anywhere.

### The Production, by the Numbers

- 85,427 words: 49,393 in the chapters, 30,969 in the annotated pool, 5,065 in the glossary and formulas.

- 40 figures, all original.

- 409 of 409 pool questions annotated — question verbatim, answer keyed, one-line why.

- 52 tooling tests and 8 audit checks, all green at ship time.

- Roughly 47 subagent launches (estimate) across the tooling, canon, figures, chapters, appendix, and audit phases.

- Calendar span: July 22 to July 23, 2026, with parallel agents throughout.

- About 3.6 million subagent tokens (estimate). The methodology, in one line: this runtime does not meter subagent tokens; the estimate models all agent reads of the canonical files plus written output volume at about four characters per token.

None of that is the point of the book. The point is the person holding it. If you finish these eleven chapters and pass your exam, the workflow did its job — and the hobby gets one more operator. Welcome.
