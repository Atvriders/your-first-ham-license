## 2. Teaching beats (ordered `###` outline)

### Beat 1 — The week before: study strategy
- You've read the book; now drill. Reread each chapter's Exam Focus; work Appendix A end to end; take timed practice exams (the repo's `tools/make_exam.py` draws a valid 35-question exam, one per group, with a separate answer key — mention it plainly as this book's companion tool).
- Test-taking strategy for a fixed public pool: every question on your exam is one you've seen; read all four choices (beware "All these choices are correct" — verify each item, never auto-pick); watch for the wrong-operation arithmetic distractors in the math questions; 26 of 35 passes, so you can miss 9 — calm beats perfect.
- Vocabulary: (none new).

### Beat 2 — Exam day: what happens at the session
- What to bring: one legal photo ID (state driver's license, passport, military ID, student school photo ID) or two forms of non-photo ID; your FRN; the session fee (ARRL VEC $15.00, $5.00 under 18 — calendar-2026 figures; other VECs vary, some are free — say "check with the team," no universal claim).
- The paperwork: the NCVEC Quick-Form 605 — you complete Section 1 (email address and FRN are mandatory); every applicant answers the Basic Qualification Question (felony conviction status; a "YES" triggers additional FCC procedures — answer honestly, it's not an automatic bar).
- The session itself: a team of at least 3 VEs; a typical ARRL VEC session tests about ten people and lasts about three and a half hours; 35 multiple-choice questions, one per pool group.
- VEs grade immediately and the VEs alone determine correctness. Pass → three VEs certify and issue your CSCE. Fail → the VEs return the application and tell you the grade.
- Vocabulary: VE, VEC, CSCE, Form 605, Basic Qualification Question.

### Beat 3 — If you don't pass (and it's fine)
- Under ARRL VEC retest policy, a failed element may be retaken at the same session only if the team has a different version of that element, has the time/resources/willingness, and the applicant pays an additional test fee.
- Nothing in FCC rules entitles a failed candidate to an immediate retest — it's entirely the VE team's decision. No shame in coming back; the pool isn't going anywhere.
- A compromised exam may never be administered, and the same question set may never be re-administered to the same examinee — that's why the retake is a different version.

### Beat 4 — After you pass: CSCE → fee → grant → call sign
- The VEs submit your application to the coordinating VEC, which screens it and forwards everything to the FCC electronically.
- The FCC emails a payment link for the $35 application fee; you have 10 calendar days from issuance of the application file number to pay (can pay sooner by looking up the pending application by FRN). Miss the window and the application is dismissed — but no retest; the VEC can refile any time before the CSCE expires. Attribute these mechanics as "the FCC process as described by ARRL" (canon §7.12).
- **Hard rule first (canon §7.11):** no transmitting until your grant appears in ULS. Then: it typically appears the next business day after payment (typical-only wording, never a promise; may add "allow a few days to about two weeks"). The FCC emails a link to the official electronic license (valid 30 days); no paper license.
- Your CSCE is valid 365 days for the element credit it conveys. Your call sign arrives with the grant. (Upgrade immediate-privileges rule §97.9(b) may be mentioned in one line as a "later, on General" teaser.)
- Vocabulary: CSCE validity, ULS grant, application file number.

### Beat 5 — Your first radio (buying guidance, brand-free)
- Category advice only (judgment call — no brand names, no prices, no "best radio" claims): a VHF/UHF FM handheld (HT) is the classic first radio — cheap, simple, works the local repeaters from ch06; check which bands your local repeaters use before buying (2 m and 70 cm cover most); the stock "rubber duck" is convenient but inefficient (ch04's lesson) — a better antenna is the highest-value upgrade.
- What else you might want later: a mobile rig for the car, a better antenna before more power, and eventually HF — which is what General unlocks.
- Where to buy: new, used (hamfests, club members), and club loaners — framed generically.
- Vocabulary: HT, mobile rig, rubber duck (recall).

### Beat 6 — `### Your 30-Day Plan` (replaces Exam Focus)
A week-by-week plan, roughly: **Week 1** — program your local repeaters into the radio (offset + tone, from ch06), listen to a net; make your first simplex contact on 146.520. **Week 2** — check into a local net (ch06 manners); introduce yourself at a club meeting. **Week 3** — try a digital mode: decode FT8 or watch APRS on a map (ch07). **Week 4** — set up your station properly (ch05), check your SWR, review RF exposure for your setup (ch09); start thinking about General. Keep each week to 3–4 concrete actions.

### Beat 7 — What's next: General and beyond
- General class opens most of HF (Elements 2 + 3) — and it's the next book in this series; Extra (Elements 2 + 3 + 4) follows. One encouraging paragraph, no hard sell.
