## 3. Teaching beats (ordered `###` outline)

Condensed from `canon/research/r3-teaching-t1-t5.md` (T3 groups). Conceptual physics, one formula — keep it that way.

### Beat 1 — What a radio wave is
- An electric field and a magnetic field at right angles, traveling together (T3B01, T3B03); polarization is defined by the orientation of the **electric** field (T3B02).
- In free space every radio wave travels at the speed of light regardless of frequency — approximately 300,000,000 m/s = 3×10⁸ m/s (T3B04, T3B11, T3B12). The myth that higher frequencies travel faster is a distractor.
- Vocabulary: electric field, magnetic field, electromagnetic wave, polarization, free space.

### Beat 2 — Frequency and wavelength: the one formula
- Wavelength and frequency are inversely related — higher frequency, shorter wavelength (T3B05).
- **λ(m) = 300 / f(MHz)** — the pool's own formula (T3B06), presented as an approximation of c = f·λ with c ≈ 3×10⁸ m/s, never as an exact identity (canon §3).
- Band names come from wavelength: "2 meters" ≈ 144–148 MHz because 300/146 ≈ 2.05; "6 meters" = 50–54 MHz because 300/50 = 6 (T3B07).
- Spectrum boundaries to memorize: HF = 3–30 MHz, VHF = 30–300 MHz, UHF = 300–3000 MHz (T3B08, T3B09, T3B10).
- Vocabulary: wavelength, frequency, hertz, HF, VHF, UHF.

### Beat 3 — Line of sight, the radio horizon, and fading
- VHF/UHF normally travel line-of-sight; UHF signals are usually not propagated by the ionosphere, so simplex UHF rarely passes the radio horizon (T3C01).
- The radio horizon lies beyond the visual horizon — the atmosphere refracts radio waves slightly (T3C11).
- Multipath — the same signal arriving over different paths, combining in or out of phase — is one mechanism behind four questions: VHF strength changing when an antenna moves a few feet (T3A01), the mobile flutter called "picket fencing" (T3A06), irregular fading on ionospheric paths (T3A08), and increased error rates on data (T3A10). Figure: `{{fig:ch03-multipath-fading}}`.
- Absorption grows with frequency: vegetation absorbs UHF/microwave energy (T3A02); precipitation decreases range at microwave frequencies (T3A07); fog and rain have little effect on 10- and 6-meter signals (T3A12).
- Figure: `{{fig:ch03-groundwave-skywave}}`.
- Vocabulary: line of sight, radio horizon, multipath, fading, picket fencing, absorption.

### Beat 4 — Polarization in practice (teach T3A04 and T3A09 together)
- Convention: horizontal polarization for long-distance CW/SSB weak-signal work on VHF/UHF (T3A03).
- On a line-of-sight path, cross-polarized antennas reduce received signal strength (T3A04).
- Ionospherically propagated signals become elliptically polarized, so on skywave paths either antenna orientation works (T3A09) — the deliberate contrast with T3A04; teach them side by side or they look contradictory.
- Blocked from a repeater by buildings? A directional antenna can find a reflected path (T3A05).
- Figure: `{{fig:ch03-polarization}}`.
- Vocabulary: horizontal/vertical/elliptical polarization, cross-polarization.

### Beat 5 — The ionosphere and HF skip
- The ionosphere is the atmospheric region that reflects HF radio waves (T3A11); stratosphere/troposphere are distractors.
- Long-distance ionospheric (skywave) propagation is far more common on HF than on VHF and above (T3C02) — HF's defining trait.
- The sunspot cycle: best long-distance 10-meter F-region propagation is dawn to shortly after sunset during high sunspot activity (T3C09); at the sunspot peak both 6 and 10 meters can deliver F-region DX (T3C10).
- Figure: `{{fig:ch03-ionosphere-skip}}`.
- Vocabulary: ionosphere, skywave, skip, F region, sunspot cycle.

### Beat 6 — VHF beyond the horizon: the exotic modes
- Sporadic E: occasional strong beyond-horizon signals on 10, 6, and 2 meters (T3C04).
- Tropospheric ducting, caused by temperature inversions (T3C08), routinely carries VHF/UHF approximately 300 miles (T3C06).
- Knife-edge diffraction bends signals over or around obstructions (T3C05); auroral backscatter returns VHF signals distorted with a characteristic raspy sound (T3C03); meteor scatter is best on 6 meters (T3C07).
- Vocabulary: sporadic E, tropospheric ducting, temperature inversion, knife-edge diffraction, auroral backscatter, meteor scatter.
