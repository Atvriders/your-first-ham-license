## 3. Teaching beats (ordered `###` outline)

Condensed from `canon/research/r4-teaching-t6-t0.md` (T8B–T8D) and `canon/research/r5-operating.md` §4–§6. The "what each mode is FOR, what the beginner will actually see and hear" chapter.

### Beat 1 — The digital zoo: what counts as digital
- Digital modes include packet radio, IEEE 802.11 (Wi-Fi), and FT8 — all of them (T8D01). Yes, Wi-Fi counts when run under amateur rules.
- CW is simply another name for a Morse code transmission (T8D09); PSK = Phase Shift Keying (T8D06); NTSC = the analog fast-scan color TV standard (T8D04).
- Vocabulary: digital mode, packet radio, CW, PSK, NTSC, fast-scan TV.

### Beat 2 — FT8 and WSJT-X: weak signals on a clock
- FT8 is designed for very low signal-to-noise operation — contacts below audibility, exchanged in timed 15-second sequences (T8D02). Figure: `{{fig:ch07-ft8-sequence}}`.
- The WSJT-X software suite (FT8's home) also supports Earth-Moon-Earth, weak-signal propagation beacons, and meteor scatter — all (T8D10).
- Practice color (r5 §4): the PC clock must be accurate to about a second; an FT8 QSO is minimal and scripted (call signs, reports, 73).
- Vocabulary: FT8, WSJT-X, signal-to-noise ratio, EME, meteor scatter.

### Beat 3 — APRS and packet radio
- APRS carries GPS position, short text messages, and weather data (T8D03); its signature use is real-time tactical digital communications with stations plotted on a map (T8D05); in North America it lives on 144.390 MHz.
- Mechanics in one breath: tracker beacons a packet → digipeaters relay → iGates push it to the internet (r5 §4). Figure: `{{fig:ch07-aprs-path}}`.
- Packet radio proper: a header with the destination station's call sign, a checksum for error detection, and ARQ — the receiving station detects errors and requests retransmission (T8D08, T8D11). The modem box is a TNC; VHF packet rides FM (bridge to ch02's T8A02).
- Vocabulary: APRS, digipeater, iGate, packet radio, TNC, checksum, header, ARQ.

### Beat 4 — DMR and mesh
- DMR (Digital Mobile Radio) time-multiplexes two digital voice signals on a single 12.5 kHz repeater channel — two "time slots" (T8D07). (Color code/talkgroup were ch06's T2B material — one-line refresher only.)
- An amateur mesh network is built from commercial Wi-Fi equipment with modified firmware, operating on amateur frequencies (T8D12).
- Vocabulary: DMR, time slot, mesh network.

### Beat 5 — Internet-linked radio
- VoIP = voice delivered over the internet using digital techniques (T8C07).
- IRLP connects amateur radio systems such as repeaters through the internet; over-the-air access to a node is by DTMF codes from your radio (T8C08, T8C06).
- EchoLink lets you operate through a repeater without a radio — from a computer or phone app (T8C09); before using it you must register your call sign and provide proof of license (T8C10).
- A gateway is an amateur station that connects other amateur stations to the internet (T8C11).
- Figure: `{{fig:ch07-echolink-topology}}`.
- Vocabulary: VoIP, IRLP, EchoLink, gateway, node.

### Beat 6 — Satellites: a repeater in the sky
- A satellite beacon is a transmission carrying status information/telemetry (T8B01, T8B05); anyone — licensed or not — may receive satellite telemetry (T8B11).
- Tracking programs take Keplerian elements as input (T8B06) and return ground-track maps, pass times with azimuth and elevation, and the Doppler-corrected frequency (T8B03).
- Doppler shift = the observed change in signal frequency caused by relative motion between the satellite and the Earth station (T8B07) — the passing-siren effect. Figure: `{{fig:ch07-doppler-curve}}`.
- U/V mode reads uplink/downlink: uplink on 70 cm, downlink on 2 m (T8B08). LEO = Low Earth Orbit, period around 100 minutes (T8B10) — the pool's exam-facing value (wording law §8). Satellites commonly use FM, SSB, and CW/data (T8B04). Spin fading = rhythmic fading from the satellite's rotation (T8B09) — normal; don't "fix" it with power.
- Power discipline: excessive uplink power can block access by other users (T8B02); the right level on a linear transponder is when your downlink is about as strong as the satellite's beacon (T8B12).
- Beginner boxes (pinned canon §2.5 values only): SO-50 — uplink 145.850 MHz FM, downlink 436.795 MHz, woken with a 2-second carrier using a 74.4 Hz tone, operated with a 67.0 Hz tone. ISS — voice downlink 145.800 MHz worldwide, VHF packet 145.825 MHz; any US amateur with a Technician class or higher license may contact the ISS (bridge to ch08's T1B02).
- Figures: `{{fig:ch07-satellite-pass}}` (AOS/LOS geometry), `{{fig:ch07-doppler-curve}}`.
- Vocabulary: telemetry, beacon, transponder, uplink/downlink, Doppler shift, Keplerian elements, satellite pass (AOS/LOS), spin fading, LEO, U/V mode.

### Beat 7 — Operating activities: contests, fox hunts, grid locators
- Contesting = contacting as many stations as possible in a specified period; good practice is to send only the minimum information needed for identification and the contest exchange (T8C03, T8C04).
- A grid locator is a letter-number designator for a geographic location (the Maidenhead system, e.g., "FN31") (T8C05).
- Radio direction finding (RDF) locates sources of noise, interference, or jamming; a directional antenna is the key tool for hidden-transmitter ("fox") hunts (T8C01, T8C02).
- Optional beginner on-ramps from r5 §6 (plainly framed as activities, not exam material): Rookie Roundup, ARRL Kids Day, Field Day.
- Vocabulary: contesting, contest exchange, grid locator (Maidenhead), RDF, fox hunt.

## 5. Worked example

The Doppler memory-channel ladder (pool-adjacent numbers from the pinned SO-50 values, r5 §5): "SO-50's downlink is 436.795 MHz, and Doppler shifts it about 10 kHz over a pass. Program three memories: at AOS listen 10 kHz **high** — 436.795 + 0.010 = **436.805 MHz**; at mid-pass, 436.795; at LOS, 10 kHz **low** — 436.795 − 0.010 = **436.785 MHz**. Your uplink stays fixed at 145.850 MHz."

## 8. Tone & integrity notes — this chapter's wording laws (canon §7.6/§7.14, binding)
