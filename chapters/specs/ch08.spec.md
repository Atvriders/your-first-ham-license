### Beat 1 — Why the service exists
- §97.1's five prongs, one plain sentence each (T1A01 keys "advancing skills in the technical and communication phases of the radio art"; noble-sounding distractors like contesting are not in §97.1).
- The FCC — not the ARRL (a private membership society), not Homeland Security — regulates and enforces the rules (T1A02).
- Willful or malicious interference is always prohibited (T1A11, §97.101(d)); at all times, emergency communications get priority (§97.101(c)).
- Vocabulary: basis and purpose, FCC, harmful interference.

### Beat 2 — License classes, grants, and call signs
- Five classes exist in the rules; the FCC currently issues new licenses only for Technician, General, Amateur Extra (T1C01 — **revised form**, see §8). Technician = Element 2 only.
- Your operating authority begins when the grant appears in ULS — a CSCE only proves you passed (T1A05, T1C10); licensing is all-electronic now (T1A04); keep your email current — revocation/suspension can result from undeliverable FCC correspondence (T1C04, §97.23).
- Three clocks: renewal window opens 90 days before expiration (T1C07); license term 10 years (T1C08); grace period 2 years after expiration — you may renew but may NOT transmit until the renewal is granted (T1C09, T1C11).
- Call-sign anatomy: prefix (K/N/W or two letters), region numeral, 1–3 letter suffix. The sequential system's four groups — Group D = 2×3 like **KF1XXX** (T1C05; see §8 for the §7.1 source rule). A Technician may hold Group C or D formats. Vanity call signs: any licensed amateur may request one (T1C02).
- Operating from a ship or aircraft requires the master's / pilot in command's approval (T1C06); international communications are limited to amateur-service purposes plus remarks of a personal character (T1C03).
- Figure: `{{fig:ch08-callsign-anatomy}}`.
- Vocabulary: operator/primary station license grant, ULS, CSCE, vanity call sign, Group D call sign, grace period, sequential call sign system.

### Beat 3 — Your privileges: bands, modes, power
- Technician VHF-and-up allocations (Region 2): 6 m = 50–54 MHz (52.525 MHz is inside — T1B03), 2 m = 144–148 MHz (146.52 is inside — T1B04), plus 1.25 m and 70 cm. Band names come from wavelength (bridge to ch03).
- Technician HF: CW-only segments on 80 m (3.525–3.600), 40 m (7.025–7.125), 15 m (21.025–21.200); on 10 m, 28.0–28.5 MHz with CW/data below and **SSB phone only 28.300–28.500** (T1B01, T1B06).
- Data emissions (e.g., FT8) authorized on 10 m, 6 m, and 2 m — all three (T1B05); SSB phone may be used in at least some segment of every amateur band above 50 MHz (T1B10); the bottoms of 6 m and 2 m (50.0–50.1, 144.0–144.1 MHz) are CW-only because CW is authorized anywhere the control operator has privileges (T1B07).
- Secondary allocations: where amateur radio is secondary you may encounter non-amateur stations and must avoid interfering with them (T1B08, §97.303).
- Never set your transmit frequency exactly at a band edge — display calibration error, sidebands spilling past the edge, and transmitter drift all apply (T1B09, §97.307(b)).
- Power: 200 W PEP in the Technician HF segments (T1B11); 1500 W PEP above 30 MHz **except for some specific restrictions** — the general ceiling, never a band-by-band grant (T1B12; wording law §8). Minimum power necessary always (§97.313(a)).
- Beacons: automatically controlled HF propagation beacons live on 10 m, 28.200–28.300 MHz (T1A06). Any US amateur with a Technician class or higher license may contact the ISS (T1B02; citations per §8).
- Figure: `{{fig:ch08-tech-band-chart}}`.
- Vocabulary: allocation, sub-band, emission mode, phone/CW/data, PEP, primary vs secondary service, band edge, beacon.

### Beat 4 — The control operator and the three control types
- A control operator is required whenever a station transmits — no exception, even with automatic control (T1E01); the control operator is the amateur designated by the station licensee to be responsible for transmissions (T1E11); the station licensee designates the control operator (T1E03).
- A station's transmitting privileges are those of the **control operator's** license class (T1E04; citations per §8); a Technician may never be control operator of a station transmitting in an Amateur Extra–only segment (T1E06); when control operator ≠ licensee, both are equally responsible (T1E07).
- The control point is the location at which the control operator function is performed (T1E05).
- Three control types: local, remote (via a control link, e.g. over the internet — T1E10; any station may be remotely controlled — T1E09), and automatic (the designated station types only; repeater operation is the canonical example — T1E08).
- Satellite eligibility: any amateur authorized to transmit on a satellite's uplink frequency may be control operator of a station communicating through it (T1E02; citations per §8).
- Vocabulary: control operator, control point, local/remote/automatic control, station licensee.

### Beat 5 — Identification
- Transmit your assigned call sign at the end of each communication and at least every 10 minutes during one (T1F03, §97.119(a)). Tactical call signs ("Race Headquarters") are allowed but never replace the FCC call sign (T1F02).
- Phone ID must be in English; phonetics encouraged, not required (T1F04, T1A03); ID may be sent by CW or phone (T1F05); self-assigned indicators may use "stroke," "slant," or "slash" — all acceptable (T1F06).
- The contrast pair: model-craft control signals are exempt from ID (with a label affixed and ≤1 W — T1D11), but on-the-air test transmissions must identify (T1D12).
- Vocabulary: station identification, tactical call sign, self-assigned indicator.

### Beat 6 — Prohibited and permitted transmissions
- Prohibited only with countries whose administration has notified the ITU that it objects (T1D01).
- Broadcasting = transmissions intended for reception by the general public (T1D10) — prohibited (T1D02, §97.113(b)); permitted one-way transmissions are an enumerated list (emergency comms, Morse practice, telecommand/telemetry…) — "announcements of ham events" is not on it.
- Messages encoded to obscure their meaning are allowed only for telecommand to space stations and control signals to model craft (T1D03); published digital codes (FT8 etc.) are fine.
- Music only when incidental to an authorized retransmission of manned spacecraft communications (T1D04) — absurdly narrow; pin it. Obscene/indecent language prohibited categorically; no FCC word list exists (T1D06).
- No pecuniary interest — but you may occasionally offer your own amateur equipment for sale, not on a regular basis (T1D05); compensation for operating only when incidental to classroom instruction at an educational institution (T1D08).
- Communications supporting broadcasting/news gathering only when directly related to the immediate safety of human life or protection of property (T1D09; cite §97.113(b) — see §8).
- Only auxiliary, repeater, or space stations may automatically retransmit other amateur stations' signals (T1D07); a repeater simultaneously retransmits on a different channel (T1F09); if a repeater inadvertently retransmits a violation, accountability falls on the originating station's control operator (T1F10).
- Third-party communications = a message from one control operator to another on behalf of a non-licensed person (T1F08); with a foreign station only where a third-party agreement exists (T1F07).
- The licensee must make the station and its records available for inspection at any time upon FCC request (T1F01); a club station grant requires at least four persons plus name, organization document, management, and purpose (T1F11).
- Frequency coordinators are volunteers recognized by local amateurs who recommend repeater/auxiliary channels — not FCC-appointed (T1A08, T1A09, §97.3(a)(22)); a space station is an amateur station more than 50 km above Earth's surface (T1A07); RACES control operators need civil-defense certification (T1A10, ties to ch06's T2C04).
- Vocabulary: broadcasting, one-way transmission, encoded to obscure meaning, pecuniary interest, third-party communications, auxiliary station, repeater, frequency coordinator, space station.
