## 2. Teaching beats (ordered `###` outline)

### Beat 1 — What ham radio is (and isn't)
- What hams actually do: talk locally on VHF/UHF, around the world on HF, experiment with electronics and software, serve in emergencies.
- The service has a stated purpose — five prongs in FCC rule §97.1 (emergency communications; advancing the radio art; advancing skills in both the communication and technical phases; a reservoir of trained operators; international goodwill). One sentence each, plain language; full treatment is ch08's job.
- Ham radio is non-commercial by rule, and anyone can get in — no electronics background assumed. Reassure the exam-anxious reader: the entire question pool is public, and this book teaches all of it.
- Vocabulary: amateur radio, Amateur Radio Service, FCC, license class.

### Beat 2 — The three license classes
- Technician → General → Amateur Extra, what each roughly adds (Technician = VHF/UHF everyday radio + HF slivers; General = most HF; Extra = everything). Figure: `{{fig:ch00-license-ladder}}`.
- **Wording law (canon §2.1 / revised T1C01):** five classes exist in the rules (Novice, Technician, General, Advanced, Amateur Extra) but the FCC currently issues new licenses only for Technician, General, and Amateur Extra. Novice/Technician Plus/Advanced are legacy. Never say the FCC issues Novice or Advanced licenses.
- Each class has its own exam element: Technician = Element 2 only; General adds Element 3; Extra adds Element 4.
- Vocabulary: Technician, General, Amateur Extra, Element 2/3/4, license class, grandfathered class.

### Beat 3 — The exam itself
- 35 questions, one drawn from each of the pool's 35 groups, out of the public 409-question 2026–2030 pool; 26 correct to pass.
- The pool is public and fixed for exams 2026-07-01 → 2030-06-30 — there are no surprise questions; every question on your exam is in Appendix A of this book, in this chapter's own words.
- Vocabulary: question pool, subelement, group, NCVEC, passing score.

### Beat 4 — Before exam day
- Get your FRN first: register in CORES with an FCC username account; the FRN is a 10-digit identifier for all your FCC business and is available immediately. **Wording law (canon §7.2):** use only the pinned sentence "Registering in CORES to get your FRN carries no fee and no exam requirement." — never print "free" / "free of charge."
- The Social Security number is given inside CORES — it does not go on the exam form.
- Find a session: ARRL's exam-session finder and hamstudy.org/sessions. Exams are run by VE teams (at least 3 VEs) under a VEC. **Wording law (canon §7.13):** remote video-supervised exams exist but availability depends entirely on the individual VE team — never promise remote testing.
- What to bring: one legal photo ID (or two non-photo IDs), your FRN, the session fee (ARRL VEC: $15.00; $5.00 under 18 — calendar-2026 figures).
- Vocabulary: FRN, CORES, VEC, VE, exam session, Form 605.

### Beat 5 — After you pass: CSCE → $35 fee → ULS grant
- The VEs grade immediately; pass and three VEs sign your CSCE. The VEC (never the VE team) forwards your application to the FCC electronically.
- The FCC emails a payment link for the $35 application fee; the candidate has 10 calendar days from issuance of the application file number to pay. Attribute the mechanics as "the FCC process as described by ARRL" (canon §7.12). VEs never collect the $35 at the session.
- **Hard rule first (canon §7.11):** no transmitting until your grant appears in the FCC's ULS database. Then: it typically appears the next business day after payment (typical-only wording, never a promise; may add "allow a few days to about two weeks").
- The FCC emails a link to the official electronic license (valid 30 days); no paper license arrives.
- Your call sign arrives with the grant, assigned by the sequential call sign system for your class and mailing address.
- Figure: `{{fig:ch00-exam-journey}}`.
- Vocabulary: CSCE, ULS, license grant, call sign, sequential call sign system.

Keep each item one line.
