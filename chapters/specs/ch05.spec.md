## 3. Teaching beats (ordered `###` outline)

Condensed from `canon/research/r3-teaching-t1-t5.md` (T4) and `canon/research/r4-teaching-t6-t0.md` (T7). This is the "what plugs into what and why" chapter — concrete and hands-on.

### Beat 1 — Anatomy of a station
- The block diagram: transceiver, power supply, mic/key, SWR meter, feed line, antenna — what each box does. Figure: `{{fig:ch05-station-block}}`.
- A transceiver combines a receiver and a transmitter in one unit (T7A02); HT vs mobile vs base as form factors, not different ideas.
- Vocabulary: transceiver, feed line, power supply, SWR meter.

### Beat 2 — Powering the station
- A typical 50-watt-output mobile FM transceiver needs a 13.8-volt supply rated around 12 amperes (T4A01) — 13.8 V is the standard vehicle/lead-acid operating voltage, and headroom is needed because transmitter efficiency is well under 100%.
- Short, heavy-gauge DC leads minimize voltage drop (T4A03); a mobile transceiver's negative return connects at the 12 V battery chassis ground (T4A11).
- Battery runtime = ampere-hour rating ÷ average current draw (T4A09 — the chapter's one formula; see §5).
- Vocabulary: ampere-hour, watt-hour, voltage drop.

### Beat 3 — Measuring as you hook up: SWR and power
- An accessory SWR meter must be rated for the frequency and power level at which you'll measure (T4A02); an RF power meter installs in the feed line between transmitter and antenna (T4A05).
- SWR readings: 1:1 = perfect match (T7C04); 4:1 = an impedance mismatch (T7C06). A directional wattmeter — reading forward and reflected power — is the instrument used to determine SWR (T7C08).
- High SWR (not low) can make a solid-state transceiver reduce output power, to protect the RF output transistors from reflected power (T7B04, T7C05) — the radio protects itself; it cannot fix the antenna.
- A dummy load is a 50-ohm non-inductive resistor on a heat sink (T7C03) for testing without putting a signal on the air (T7C01); an antenna analyzer tells you whether an antenna is resonant at your operating frequency (T7C02).
- Figure: `{{fig:ch05-swr-hookup}}`.
- Vocabulary: SWR meter, directional wattmeter, forward/reflected power, dummy load, non-inductive resistor, antenna analyzer.

### Beat 4 — Computers, digital interfaces, and small accessories
- A digital-mode interface moves three signals: receive audio, transmit audio, transmitter keying (T4A06); concretely, the transceiver's speaker connector feeds the computer's line-in (T4A07); FT8 audio flows between the radio and the computer running the software (T4A04).
- A digital hotspot is a small RF gateway connecting nearby transceivers to an internet digital voice/data network (T4A10); an electronic keyer forms Morse dits and dahs for you (T4A12).
- Flat copper strap is the preferred RF bonding conductor — low inductance at RF (T4A08).
- Vocabulary: hotspot, electronic keyer, keying/PTT, bonding, AFSK.

### Beat 5 — The operating controls
- Enter frequency with keypad or VFO knob (T4B02); scanning tunes through a range checking for activity (T4B05).
- Squelch mutes receiver audio when no signal is present; to hear a weak FM signal, set the threshold so audio is on all the time — squelch fully open (T4B03); an FM signal slightly off frequency sounds distorted (T4B04).
- Excessive microphone gain on SSB produces distorted transmitted audio (T4B01) — the SSB cousin of FM overdeviation (ch06's T2B05).
- SSB voice pitch too high or low? Adjust the RIT (Clarifier) — it nudges receive frequency without moving your transmit frequency (T4B06).
- Filter bandwidth matched to mode reduces noise; among the pool's choices, 2400 Hz gives the best signal-to-noise ratio for SSB (T4B08, T4B10).
- Digital radio config: a DMR code plug is the configuration data loaded into the radio (T4B07); a group of stations is selected by entering the group's identification code (T4B09); a D-STAR radio must have your call sign programmed before it can transmit (T4B11).
- Vocabulary: VFO, squelch, scanning, RIT/Clarifier, filter bandwidth, microphone gain, code plug, talkgroup.

### Beat 6 — Inside the radio
- Sensitivity = ability to detect weak signals (T7A01); selectivity = ability to discriminate between nearby signals (T7A04) — detect vs separate, the most-missed pair.
- Mixer converts a signal from one frequency to another (T7A03); oscillator generates a signal at a specific frequency (T7A05); the VFO sets a transceiver's receive and transmit frequency (T7A11).
- A transverter converts a transceiver's RF input and output to another band (T7A06); the PTT input switches from receive to transmit **when grounded** (T7A07); modulation = combining speech (or data) with an RF carrier (T7A08).
- The SSB / CW-FM switch on some VHF power amplifiers **sets the amplifier for proper operation in the selected mode** (T7A09 — quote the *revised* wording, see §8); to raise output power, add an RF power amplifier after the transceiver (T7A10).
- Vocabulary: sensitivity, selectivity, mixer, oscillator, VFO, transverter, PTT, modulation, RF power amplifier.

### Beat 7 — When things interfere (RFI)
- Overdeviation on FM: talking too loudly/close swings the carrier too far — the fix for audio that drops out on voice peaks is to talk farther away from the microphone (T7B01; distorted audio through a repeater can also come from being off frequency or a bad location — T7B10).
- A broadcast radio/TV that picks up your transmission is being overloaded by a strong signal it cannot reject (fundamental overload) — the problem is in the affected receiver (T7B02); RFI can come from fundamental overload, harmonics, or spurious emissions — all three (T7B03).
- Filter direction logic: your clean signal bothers a neighbor's receiver → filter at the affected receiver's antenna input (T7B05); a nearby commercial FM station overloads your 2 m rig → band-reject filter at your receiver (T7B07); cable TV interference (non-fiber) → first check all coax connectors are properly installed (T7B09).
- Neighbor protocol: first verify your own station is operating properly (T7B06); if their device interferes with you, work with them, note FCC rules prohibit interference-causing devices, keep your own station above reproach (T7B08).
- A clip-on ferrite choke on the microphone cable stops transmitted RF from feeding back into the rig (T7B11).
- Figure: `{{fig:ch05-rfi-path}}`.
- Vocabulary: overdeviation, fundamental overload, harmonic, spurious emission, band-reject filter, ferrite choke, RF feedback.

### Beat 8 — The workbench: feed-line health, meters, soldering
- Power lost in a feed line is converted into heat (T7C07); coax enemies: moisture contamination is the classic killer (T7C09), sunlight cracks the jacket and lets water in — jackets must be UV-resistant (T7C10), foam-dielectric coax has less loss per foot than solid (T7C11).
- Voltmeter measures voltage and connects **in parallel** (T7D01, T7D02); ammeter measures current and connects **in series** (T7D03, T7D04); a multimeter measures voltage and resistance (and current) — not impedance or signal strength (T7D07).
- An ohmmeter measures resistance using its own internal battery — never on a powered circuit (T7D05, T7D11); across a large discharged capacitor it reads increasing resistance with time as its battery charges the capacitor (T7D10); measuring voltage while set to the resistance setting can damage the meter (T7D06).
- Soldering: rosin-core for electronics, never acid-core (T7D08); a cold solder joint looks rough or lumpy — a good joint is shiny and smooth (T7D09).
- Vocabulary: feed-line loss, dielectric (foam vs solid), voltmeter, ammeter, ohmmeter, multimeter, rosin-core/acid-core solder, cold solder joint.

## 5. Worked example

## 8. Tone & integrity notes

- Absolute-beginner audience; concrete and hands-on; this is the chapter where the reader mentally assembles their first station.
- **Revised-question law:** T7A09 exists only in its 2026-02-19 revised form — "What is the function of the switch which selects either SSB or CW-FM on some VHF power amplifiers?" keyed "Set the amplifier for proper operation in the selected mode". Quote and teach the revised form (canon §1.3); the key intuition: the operator picks the mode, the switch adapts the amplifier — it does not change your signal's mode.
- Squelch direction reads backwards to beginners (T4B03) — explain it slowly.
- "All these choices are correct" is keyed several times in T7 (T7B03, T7B08, T7B10) but is also a trap elsewhere (T7B06, T7D07, T7A10) — teach students to verify each option, never auto-pick it (r4 cross-cutting note).
