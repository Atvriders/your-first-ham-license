### Beat 1 — The RF spectrum and where hams live
- Radio waves are a slice of the electromagnetic spectrum; hams get specific bands (allocations) from HF through microwave.
- Orient the reader: HF (3–30 MHz), VHF (30–300 MHz), UHF (300–3000 MHz) — named now, drilled in ch03; Technician-relevant bands marked on the figure.
- Figure: `{{fig:ch02-rf-spectrum}}`.
- Vocabulary: spectrum, band, allocation, HF, VHF, UHF.

### Beat 2 — Carriers and the mode families
- A carrier is a steady RF signal; modulation combines speech or data with it. Three families the pool cares about: **AM** varies the carrier's amplitude; **FM/PM** vary its frequency/phase; **CW** is just a carrier keyed on and off (Morse).
- SSB is a form of AM (T8A01) — an AM signal with the carrier and one sideband removed.
- Figure: `{{fig:ch02-am-fm-cw}}`.
- Vocabulary: carrier, modulation, AM, FM, PM, CW, sideband, SSB.

### Beat 3 — Who uses what (and why)
- FM (or PM): VHF/UHF voice repeaters (T8A04) and VHF packet radio (T8A02) — the everyday local mode.
- SSB: long-distance weak-signal voice on VHF/UHF (T8A03) and on HF — the DX mode.
- Convention: **upper sideband (USB)** on 10-meter HF and on VHF/UHF (T8A06). (May add as orientation, not exam material: LSB is the convention below 10 MHz.)
- Vocabulary: repeater (forward reference — ch06 explains), DX, USB/LSB, weak-signal.

### Beat 4 — Bandwidth: the ladder to memorize
- Bandwidth = how much spectrum a signal occupies. The four pool values, anchored as a ladder: **CW ≈ 150 Hz** (T8A11 — the narrowest common emission, T8A05) < **SSB voice ≈ 3 kHz** (T8A08) < **FM voice on VHF repeaters ≈ 10–15 kHz** (T8A09) < **AM fast-scan TV ≈ 6 MHz** (T8A10).
- SSB's advantage over FM is its narrower bandwidth (T8A07) — why SSB is the weak-signal mode. Figure: `{{fig:ch02-ssb-vs-am-spectrum}}`.
- Vocabulary: bandwidth, sideband, fast-scan TV.

### Beat 5 — FM's catch: the capture effect
- FM's tested weakness: only the strongest signal is received at a time (T8A12). Plain-language intuition: an FM receiver "captures" the loudest signal on the channel — great for local repeaters, useless for digging out weak DX.
- Vocabulary: capture effect.
