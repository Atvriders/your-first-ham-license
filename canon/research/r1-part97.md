## 1. Basis and purpose of the Amateur Radio Service (T1A01, T1A02)

- **FACT:** The basis and purpose of the Amateur Radio Service has exactly five prongs, the first being emergency communications: "Recognition and enhancement of the value of the amateur service to the public as a voluntary noncommercial communication service, particularly with respect to providing emergency communications." — Source: §97.1(a), quoted verbatim.
- **FACT:** Prong two: "Continuation and extension of the amateur's proven ability to contribute to the advancement of the radio art." — Source: §97.1(b), verbatim.
- **FACT:** Prong three: "Encouragement and improvement of the amateur service through rules which provide for advancing skills in both the communication and technical phases of the art." — Source: §97.1(c), verbatim. (This is the wording T1A01's correct answer paraphrases: "advancing skills in the technical and communication phases of the radio art.")
- **FACT:** Prong four: "Expansion of the existing reservoir within the amateur radio service of trained operators, technicians, and electronics experts." — Source: §97.1(d), verbatim.
- **FACT:** Prong five: "Continuation and extension of the amateur's unique ability to enhance international goodwill." — Source: §97.1(e), verbatim.
- **FACT:** The FCC is the agency that regulates and enforces the Amateur Radio Service rules in the United States; Part 97 is the FCC's own rule part ("The rules and regulations in this part are designed to provide an amateur radio service…") and §97.3(a)(21) defines "FCC" as the Federal Communications Commission. — Source: §97.1 preamble; §97.3(a)(21).

## 2. Part 97 definitions the pool tests verbatim (§97.3)

- **FACT:** A space station is "an amateur station located more than 50 km above the Earth's surface." — Source: §97.3(a)(41), verbatim (T1A07).
- **FACT:** A frequency coordinator is "an entity, recognized in a local or regional area by amateur operators whose stations are eligible to be auxiliary or repeater stations, that recommends transmit/receive channels and associated operating and technical parameters for such stations in order to avoid or minimize potential interference." — Source: §97.3(a)(22), verbatim (T1A08, T1A09: coordinators are selected/recognized by local amateurs, not appointed by the FCC).
- **FACT:** A control operator is "an amateur operator designated by the licensee of a station to be responsible for the transmissions from that station to assure compliance with the FCC Rules." — Source: §97.3(a)(13), verbatim (T1E11).
- **FACT:** A control point is "the location at which the control operator function is performed." — Source: §97.3(a)(14), verbatim (T1E05).
- **FACT:** Automatic control is "the use of devices and procedures for control of a station when it is transmitting so that compliance with the FCC Rules is achieved without the control operator being present at a control point." — Source: §97.3(a)(6), verbatim (T1E08).
- **FACT:** Remote control is "the use of a control operator who indirectly manipulates the operating adjustments in the station through a control link to achieve compliance with the FCC Rules." — Source: §97.3(a)(39), verbatim (T1E10: operating a station over the internet is remote control).
- **FACT:** Broadcasting is defined as "transmissions intended for reception by the general public, either direct or relayed." — Source: §97.3(a)(10), verbatim (T1D10).
- **FACT:** Third-party communications are "a message from the control operator (first party) of an amateur station to another amateur station control operator (second party) on behalf of another person (third party)." — Source: §97.3(a)(47), verbatim (T1F08).
- **FACT:** A repeater is "an amateur station that simultaneously retransmits the transmission of another amateur station on a different channel or channels." — Source: §97.3(a)(40), verbatim (T1F09).
- **FACT:** A beacon is "an amateur station transmitting communications for the purposes of observation of propagation and reception or other related experimental activities." — Source: §97.3(a)(9), verbatim.
- **FACT:** An auxiliary station is "an amateur station, other than in a message forwarding system, that is transmitting communications point-to-point within a system of cooperating amateur stations." — Source: §97.3(a)(7), verbatim (T1D07).
- **FACT:** Peak envelope power (PEP) is "the average power supplied to the antenna transmission line by a transmitter during one RF cycle at the crest of the modulation envelope taken under normal operating conditions." — Source: §97.3(b)(9), verbatim (needed for T1B11/T1B12).
- **FACT:** Part 97 names nine emission types — CW, data, image, MCW, phone, pulse, RTTY, SS, test — defined by three-symbol designators per §2.201; "phone" covers "speech and other sound emissions" ending in symbol E, and "data" covers "telemetry, telecommand and computer communications emissions." — Source: §97.3(c) preamble, (c)(2), (c)(5), pinpoint paraphrase (underlies T1B05 "digital modes such as FT8" = data emission).

## 3. License grants, ULS, call signs, term and renewal (T1A04, T1A05, T1C)

- **FACT:** An amateur station may transmit only when its apparatus is under the physical control of "a person named in an amateur station license grant on the ULS consolidated license database" (or an authorized reciprocal operator) — including aboard any vessel or craft documented or registered in the United States within 50 km of the Earth's surface. — Source: §97.5(a), (a)(2), verbatim (T1C06, T1C10).
- **FACT:** Every transmitting amateur station must have a control operator "for whom an amateur operator/primary station license grant appears on the ULS consolidated licensee database" (or an alien reciprocal operator) — appearance in the FCC ULS database is what proves the grant and authorizes operation. — Source: §97.7, §97.7(a), verbatim (T1A05, T1C10).
- **FACT:** Installation and operation of an amateur station on a ship or aircraft "must be approved by the master of the ship or pilot in command of the aircraft." — Source: §97.11(a), verbatim (T1C06).
- **FACT:** The classes of amateur operator license grants are "Novice, Technician, General, Advanced, and Amateur Extra," but "no new license grant will be issued for a Novice or Advanced Class operator/primary station" — so the FCC currently issues new licenses only for Technician, General, and Amateur Extra. — Source: §97.9(a), §97.17(a), verbatim (T1C01).
- **FACT:** Any licensed amateur may apply for a vanity call sign: "the person named in an operator/primary station license grant or in a club station license grant is eligible to make application… to show a call sign selected by the vanity call sign system." — Source: §97.19(a), verbatim (T1C02).
- **FACT:** An amateur service license "is normally granted for a 10-year term." — Source: §97.25, verbatim (T1C08).
- **FACT:** Renewal may be filed no earlier than 90 days before expiration: wireless-service renewal applications "must be filed no later than the expiration date of the authorization, and no sooner than 90 days prior to the expiration date." — Source: 47 CFR §1.949(a) (the pool tags §97.21 + §1.949; §97.21(a)(3) applies §§1.913 and 1.949), verbatim (T1C07).
- **FACT:** An expired license may be renewed "during a 2 year filing grace period," but "unless and until the license grant is renewed, no privileges in this part are conferred" — no transmitting during the grace period. — Source: §97.21(b), verbatim (T1C09, T1C11).
- **FACT:** Every license grant must show the grantee's correct name, mailing address, and email address, and "revocation of the station license or suspension of the operator license may result when correspondence from the FCC is returned as undeliverable because the grantee failed to provide the correct email address." — Source: §97.23, verbatim (T1C04; see Watch item on T1A04).
- **FACT:** A club station license grant requires a trustee holding an operator/primary grant and "the club must be composed of at least four persons and must have a name, a document of organization, management, and a primary purpose devoted to amateur service activities consistent with this part." — Source: §97.5(b)(2), verbatim (T1F11).

## 4. Control operator rules and station responsibility (T1E, T2C01)

- **FACT:** An amateur station may never transmit without a control operator: "When transmitting, each amateur station must have a control operator." — Source: §97.7, verbatim (T1E01 — the "never" answer).
- **FACT:** The station licensee must designate the control operator: "The station licensee must designate the station control operator. The FCC will presume that the station licensee is also the control operator, unless documentation to the contrary is in the station records." — Source: §97.103(b), verbatim (T1E03).
- **FACT:** A station's transmitting privileges are those of its control operator: "A station may only be operated in the manner and to the extent permitted by the privileges authorized for the class of operator license held by the control operator." — Source: §97.105(b), verbatim (T1E04 — pool tags §97.103(b); §97.105(b) is the on-point text).
- **FACT:** When the control operator is not the licensee, both are responsible: "When the control operator is a different amateur operator than the station licensee, both persons are equally responsible for proper operation of the station." — Source: §97.103(a), verbatim (T1E07).
- **FACT:** Part 97 rules always apply to an amateur station — the licensee "is responsible for the proper operation of the station in accordance with the FCC Rules," with no exception for RACES/ARES/FEMA operations. — Source: §97.103(a), verbatim (T2C01, the only non-T1 tagged question).
- **FACT:** A Technician may never (emergencies aside) be the control operator of a station operating in an Amateur Extra–only segment, because §97.301 gives Technician control operators only the bands listed for their class. — Source: §97.301(a)–(e) band tables + §97.105(b), pinpoint paraphrase (T1E06).
- **FACT:** Any amateur station may be remotely controlled ("Any station may be remotely controlled"), but only stations specifically designated in Part 97 (e.g., repeaters, beacons, auxiliary, space stations) may be automatically controlled. — Source: §97.109(c), §97.109(d), verbatim/paraphrase (T1E09).
- **FACT:** A repeater may be automatically controlled. — Source: §97.205(d), verbatim (T1E08's pairing of automatic control with repeater operation).
- **FACT:** The licensee must "make the station and the station records available for inspection upon request by an FCC representative" — at any time upon request, no warrant or advance notice needed. — Source: §97.103(c), verbatim (T1F01).

## 5. Station identification (T1A03, T1D11, T1D12, T1F02–T1F06)

- **FACT:** Every amateur station (except a space or telecommand station) must transmit its assigned call sign "at the end of each communication, and at least every 10 minutes during a communication." — Source: §97.119(a), verbatim (T1F03; same rule governs tactical call signs, T1F02 — a tactical call never substitutes for the assigned call sign).
- **FACT:** "No station may transmit unidentified communications or signals" — so on-the-air test transmissions must be identified like any other transmission. — Source: §97.119(a), verbatim; brief adjustment transmissions are the one-way type authorized by §97.111(b)(1) (T1D12).
- **FACT:** Phone-emission identification must be "in the English language," and "use of a phonetic alphabet as an aid for correct station identification is encouraged" — encouraged, not required. — Source: §97.119(b)(2), verbatim (T1A03, T1F04).
- **FACT:** The required ID may be sent "by a CW emission" (automatic-device keying ≤ 20 wpm) or "by a phone emission in the English language" — either CW or phone satisfies phone-operation ID. — Source: §97.119(b)(1)–(2), verbatim (T1F05).
- **FACT:** Self-assigned indicators are permitted if "separated from the call sign by the slant mark (/) or by any suitable word that denotes the slant mark," and they must not conflict with FCC indicators or another country's prefix — so "stroke," "slant," and "slash" are all acceptable spoken separators. — Source: §97.119(c), verbatim (T1F06).
- **FACT:** Transmissions directed only to controlling a model craft are exempt from station ID provided a label with the call sign and licensee name/address is affixed to the transmitter; control signals to model craft are not considered obscuring codes, and transmitter power must not exceed 1 W. — Source: §97.215(a), (b), (c), verbatim (T1D11; also supports T1D03).

## 6. Authorized and prohibited transmissions (T1A11, T1D)

- **FACT:** "No amateur operator shall willfully or maliciously interfere with or cause interference to any radio communication or signal." — Source: §97.101(d), verbatim (T1A11).
- **FACT:** Amateur stations may not exchange communications with any country "whose administration has notified the ITU that it objects to such communications." — Source: §97.111(a)(1), verbatim (T1D01).
- **FACT:** "An amateur station shall not engage in any form of broadcasting, nor may an amateur station transmit one-way communications except as specifically provided in these rules." — Source: §97.113(b), verbatim (T1D02; one-way exceptions are enumerated in §97.111(b): brief adjustments, establishing two-way contact, telecommand, emergency communications, Morse practice, information bulletins, telemetry).
- **FACT:** Amateur stations may not transmit "messages encoded for the purpose of obscuring their meaning, except as otherwise provided herein"; the two tested exceptions are telecommand stations, which "may transmit special codes intended to obscure the meaning of telecommand messages" to space stations (§97.211(b)), and model-craft control signals (§97.215(b)). — Source: §97.113(a)(4), §97.211(b), §97.215(b), verbatim (T1D03).
- **FACT:** Music using a phone emission is prohibited except as specifically provided — the sole tested exception is incidental music in retransmissions of "communications… originating on United States Government frequencies between a manned spacecraft and its associated Earth stations," which require prior NASA approval. — Source: §97.113(a)(4), §97.113(c), verbatim (T1D04).
- **FACT:** An amateur operator "may notify other amateur operators of the availability for sale or trade of apparatus normally used in an amateur station, provided that such activity is not conducted on a regular basis." — Source: §97.113(a)(3)(ii), verbatim (T1D05).
- **FACT:** "Obscene or indecent words or language" are prohibited in any amateur transmission — there is no FCC word list; the prohibition is categorical. — Source: §97.113(a)(4), verbatim (T1D06).
- **FACT:** A control operator "may accept compensation as an incident of a teaching position during periods of time when an amateur station is used by that teacher as a part of classroom instruction at an educational institution" — the only tested case where compensation for operating is allowed. — Source: §97.113(a)(3)(iii), verbatim (T1D08; baseline rule: no "communications for hire or for material compensation," §97.113(a)(2)).
- **FACT:** Communications "directly related to the immediate safety of human life or the protection of property" may be provided to broadcasters "where no other means of communication is reasonably available before or at the time of the event" — otherwise no amateur activity related to program production or news gathering for broadcast. — Source: §97.113(b), verbatim (T1D09; pool tag "[97.113(5)(b)]" is a citation typo — see Watch items).
- **FACT:** Only auxiliary, repeater, or space stations may automatically retransmit the signals of other amateur stations. — Source: §97.113(d), verbatim (T1D07 context; auxiliary stations "may transmit one-way communications," §97.201(e)).
- **FACT:** International communications are "limited to communications incidental to the purposes of the amateur service and to remarks of a personal character." — Source: §97.117, verbatim (T1C03).
- **FACT:** Third-party traffic to a foreign station is allowed only when that country's "administration has made arrangements with the United States to allow amateur stations to be used for transmitting international communications on behalf of third parties." — Source: §97.115(a)(2), verbatim (T1F07).

## 7. Technician frequency privileges, emissions, and power (T1B)

- **FACT:** Technician control operators share the same VHF-and-up allocations as General/Advanced/Extra: in ITU Region 2 these include 6 m = 50–54 MHz, 2 m = 144–148 MHz, 1.25 m = 219–220 and 222–225 MHz, and 70 cm = 420–450 MHz. — Source: §97.301(a) table (Region 2 column), verbatim values (T1B03: 52.525 MHz is in 6 m; T1B04: 146.52 MHz is in 2 m).
- **FACT:** A Technician's HF segments are 80 m: 3.525–3.600 MHz; 40 m: 7.025–7.125 MHz; 15 m: 21.025–21.200 MHz; and 10 m: 28.0–28.5 MHz — the same segments for Novice and Technician. — Source: §97.301(e) table, verbatim values.
- **FACT:** On 80/40/15 m those Technician segments are CW-only for the Technician: "a station having a control operator holding a Novice or Technician Class operator license may only transmit a CW emission using the international Morse code." — Source: §97.307(f)(9) (applied to the 80 m, 40 m, and 15 m RTTY/data rows of §97.305(c)(3)), verbatim (T1B06: no phone on those bands).
- **FACT:** Technician phone operation on HF exists only on 10 m, 28.300–28.500 MHz, where Novice/Technician control operators "may only transmit a CW emission using the international Morse code or phone emissions J3E and R3E" (SSB). — Source: §97.305(c)(3)(xviii) + §97.307(f)(10), verbatim (T1B01, T1B06).
- **FACT:** Data emissions (the FT8 family of digital modes) are authorized on 10 m (28.0–28.3 MHz, RTTY/data), on 6 m (50.1–54 MHz) and on 2 m (144.1–148 MHz) — so Technicians have digital-mode privileges on all three bands. — Source: §97.305(c)(3)(xvii), (c)(4)(i)–(ii), (c)(4)(iii), verbatim values (T1B05).
- **FACT:** CW is authorized everywhere a control operator has privileges — "an amateur station may transmit a CW emission on any frequency authorized to the control operator" — while the §97.305(c) tables begin phone/data at 50.1 MHz (6 m) and 144.1 MHz (2 m), making 50.0–50.1 MHz and 144.0–144.1 MHz CW-only segments. — Source: §97.305(a), §97.305(c)(4)(i), (iii), verbatim (T1B07).
- **FACT:** SSB phone is authorized in at least some segment of every amateur band above 50 MHz (6 m: 50.1 MHz up; 2 m: 144.1 MHz up; 1.25 m: 222–225 MHz; 70 cm and higher: entire band). — Source: §97.305(c)(4)–(7) tables, verbatim values (T1B10).
- **FACT:** Where the amateur service is secondary, "a station in a secondary service must not cause harmful interference to, and must accept interference from, stations in a primary service" — amateurs may encounter non-amateur stations there and must avoid interfering with them. — Source: §97.303 introductory text, verbatim (T1B08).
- **FACT:** "Emissions resulting from modulation must be confined to the band or segment available to the control operator," and operation must follow "good engineering and good amateur practice" — the rule basis for staying off exact band edges (calibration error, sideband splatter, drift). — Source: §97.307(b), §97.101(a), verbatim (T1B09; pool tags §97.101(a) + §97.301).
- **FACT:** "An amateur station must use the minimum transmitter power necessary to carry out the desired communications." — Source: §97.313(a), verbatim.
- **FACT:** Technician HF power limit: no station may exceed 200 W PEP on the 3.525–3.60, 7.025–7.125, 21.025–21.20, and 28.0–28.5 MHz segments when the control operator is a Novice or Technician. — Source: §97.313(c)(2), verbatim (T1B11).
- **FACT:** The general amateur power ceiling is 1.5 kW PEP — "no station may transmit with a transmitter power exceeding 1.5 kW PEP" — which is the Technician limit above 30 MHz except where specific restrictions apply (e.g., 50 W PEP on 219–220 MHz per §97.313(h); 70 cm/33 cm geographic limits per §97.313(f)–(g)). — Source: §97.313(b), verbatim (T1B12).

## 8. Repeaters, beacons, satellites, and special stations (T1A06, T1B02, T1E02, T1F09–T1F10)

- **FACT:** Automatically controlled beacons on HF operate on 10 m in the 28.20–28.30 MHz segment (other automatic-beacon segments: 50.06–50.08, 144.275–144.300, 222.05–222.06, 432.300–432.400 MHz). — Source: §97.203(d), verbatim (T1A06).
- **FACT:** Any class of licensee may be the control operator of an Earth station or space station, "subject to the privileges of the class of operator license held by the control operator" — so any amateur allowed to transmit on a satellite uplink (e.g., the 144–146 MHz space-station segment, §97.207(c)(2)) may work the ISS, Technician included, no NASA approval required by Part 97. — Source: §97.209(a), §97.207(a), verbatim (T1B02, T1E02; see Watch items on the pool's §97.207(c) tag).
- **FACT:** When two repeaters interfere, the licensees share responsibility "unless the operation of one station is recommended by a frequency coordinator and the operation of the other station is not. In that case, the licensee of the non-coordinated repeater has primary responsibility to resolve the interference." — Source: §97.205(c), verbatim (coordination is voluntary but shifts the interference-resolution burden).
- **FACT:** "The control operator of a repeater that retransmits inadvertently communications that violate the rules in this part is not accountable for the violative communications" — accountability falls on the originating station's control operator. — Source: §97.205(g), verbatim (T1F10).
- **FACT:** Auxiliary stations are limited to "the 2 m and shorter wavelength bands" (minus listed segments) and require a Technician-class-or-higher control operator. — Source: §97.201(b), §97.201(a), verbatim (T1D07 context).

## 9. Emergency communications and RACES (T1A10, T2C context)

- **FACT:** "At all times and on all frequencies, each control operator must give priority to stations providing emergency communications" (except RACES training drills and tests). — Source: §97.101(c), verbatim.
- **FACT:** "No provision of these rules prevents the use by an amateur station of any means of radiocommunication at its disposal to provide essential communication needs in connection with the immediate safety of human life and immediate protection of property when normal communication systems are not available." — Source: §97.403, verbatim.
- **FACT:** A station in distress may use "any means at its disposal to attract attention, make known its condition and location, and obtain assistance." — Source: §97.405(a), verbatim.
- **FACT:** To be the control operator of a RACES station you must hold an FCC-issued amateur operator license and be "certified by a civil defense organization as enrolled in that organization." — Source: §97.407(a), verbatim (T1A10).
- **FACT:** RACES drills and tests "may not exceed a total time of 1 hour per week," except up to 72 hours no more than twice per calendar year with state approval. — Source: §97.407(d)(4), verbatim.
- **FACT:** A concrete secondary-sharing example the pool's §97.303 tag rests on: "Amateur stations transmitting in the 70 cm band… must not cause harmful interference to, and must accept interference from, stations authorized by the United States Government in the radiolocation service." — Source: §97.303(b), verbatim (T1B08 context).

## 10. Examinations and the VE system (§97.501–527 — book context; the 2026–2030 pool does not tag these)

- **FACT:** The Technician class license requires passing examination Element 2 only. — Source: §97.501(c), verbatim.
- **FACT:** Element 2 is "35 questions concerning the privileges of a Technician Class operator license. The minimum passing score is 26 questions answered correctly." — Source: §97.503(a), verbatim.
- **FACT:** "Each examination for an amateur operator license must be administered by a team of at least 3 VEs at an examination session coordinated by a VEC." — Source: §97.509(a), verbatim.
- **FACT:** To administer a Technician exam, each VE must be accredited, at least 18 years old, and hold an "Amateur Extra, Advanced or General Class" license; an examinee who passes must be issued a CSCE. — Source: §97.509(b), (b)(3)(i), (l), verbatim.
- **FACT:** A CSCE gives upgrade privileges for 365 days: a licensee with a pending Form 605 and a CSCE for a higher class "is authorized to exercise the rights and privileges of the higher operator class until final disposition of the application or until 365 days following the passing of the examination, whichever comes first." — Source: §97.9(b), verbatim.
- **FACT:** Every question set must use questions from the applicable pool, and "each question pool must contain at least 10 times the number of questions required for a single examination… published and made available to the public prior to its use for making a question set." — Source: §97.507(b), §97.523, verbatim.
- **FACT:** After a successful session the VEC must "forward electronically all required data to the FCC" for qualified examinees — this electronic path is why the ULS database entry (not the CSCE) is the operative grant. — Source: §97.519(b)(3), verbatim.
- **FACT:** "VEs and VECs may be reimbursed by examinees for out-of-pocket expenses incurred in preparing, processing, administering, or coordinating an examination." — Source: §97.527, verbatim.
- **FACT:** Each written question set must be prepared by a VE holding an Amateur Extra Class license — except Element 3 sets, which an Advanced Class VE may prepare, and Element 2 sets, which an Advanced or General Class VE may prepare. — Source: §97.507(a), verbatim/paraphrase.
- **FACT:** Exam credit rules: an unexpired (or in-grace-period) Technician granted on or after March 21, 1987 earns Element 2 credit; expired beyond the grace period earns none; and VEs must credit any element a CSCE shows as passed "within the previous 365 days." — Source: §97.505(a), (b), verbatim/paraphrase.
- **FACT:** No organization may serve as a VEC "unless it has entered into a written agreement with the FCC," and VECs must register every qualified examinee "without regard to race, sex, religion, national origin or membership (or lack thereof) in any amateur service organization." — Source: §97.521, verbatim.
- **FACT:** "Each examinee must comply with the instructions given by the administering VEs," and the VEs "must immediately terminate the examination upon failure of the examinee to comply with their instructions." — Source: §97.511, §97.509(c), verbatim.
- **FACT:** The FCC may readminister any examination and "cancel the operator/primary station license of any licensee who fails to appear for readministration… or who does not successfully complete any required element that is readministered." — Source: §97.519(d), verbatim (enforcement basic).

## 11. Enforcement and inspection basics the pool touches

- **FACT:** Enforcement levers named in the tested rules: revocation of the station license or suspension of the operator license for an undeliverable email address (§97.23, T1C04); mandatory station/records inspection on FCC request (§97.103(c), T1F01); FCC-ordered restricted operation hours for broadcast interference (§97.121(a)); license cancellation for failing an FCC-ordered exam readministration (§97.519(d)). — Source: sections as cited, pinpoint paraphrase.
- **FACT:** RF-safety regulation context for T0C (untagged in the pool): before transmitting, the licensee must ensure the station will not expose people to RF fields above the §1.1310 limits, must take action to prevent exposure if a routine evaluation shows excess, and may use OET Bulletin 65 methodologies — i.e., the station licensee is responsible for RF-exposure compliance and must re-evaluate when the station changes. — Source: §97.13(c)(1)–(2), pinpoint paraphrase (T0C06, T0C09, T0C13).

## Watch items — where the pool and Part 97 can confuse a beginner

- **Band plans are conventions, not rules.** T2A/T2B test repeater offsets (±600 kHz on 2 m, T2A01), calling frequencies, and simplex channels "designated in the VHF/UHF band plans" (T2B09) with no `[97.x]` tags. Part 97 contains no band plan; the only rule anchors are §97.101(a) (good engineering/amateur practice), §97.101(b) ("No frequency will be assigned for the exclusive use of any station" — the basis for T2B08's "negotiate" answer), and §97.3(a)(22) (frequency coordinators merely *recommend*). Chapters must present band plans as voluntary community practice that rules encourage, never as FCC mandates.
- **T1A04 (license notification by email).** §97.23 pins only the duty to keep a working email address on the grant and the revocation/suspension consequence. The pool's answer — "Email from the FCC with a link to download the license grant" — describes current FCC ULS practice, which is not spelled out in Part 97 text. Pinnable to §97.23 for the exam's purpose, but quote the rule, not the practice.
- **T1D09's tag "[97.113(5)(b)]" is a citation typo in the published pool** (preserved verbatim in `canon/pool-technician.txt`). The operative text is §97.113(b) (no broadcasting-related activity except immediate safety of life/property when no other means is available). There is no §97.113(5)(b).
- **T1B02/T1E02 tag §97.207(c)** (frequency bands authorized to *space stations*), but the eligibility fact — any license class may be control operator subject to privileges — lives in §97.207(a) (space stations) and §97.209(a) (Earth stations; an operator contacting the ISS runs an Earth station). Cite both to avoid implying only Extra-class hams may use satellites.
- **T1C05 (Group D call sign KF1XXX) has no `[97.x]` tag and is not pinnable to Part 97.** §97.3(a)(11) defines the sequential call sign system but the "Group A–D" call-sign formats come from FCC sequential-call-sign-system public notices, not the rule text. `UNVERIFIED:` the Group D 2×3 format claim (KF1XXX valid for Technician) cannot be pinned to a primary source fetched for this note — needs the FCC's call-sign-group public notice (or §2.106-adjacent numbering material); Part 97 is silent.
- **T1B12's "1500 watts above 30 MHz" is the general ceiling, not a band-by-band grant.** §97.313(b) sets 1.5 kW PEP, but §97.313(d)–(h) impose lower limits in specific bands/places (e.g., 50 W PEP on 219–220 MHz; 50 W PEP on 70 cm near certain military bases). The pool's own wording ("except for some specific restrictions") matches the rule — keep that caveat when quoting.
- **CW/phone "sub-bands" are emission rules, not band plans.** Beginners often think 50.0–50.1 MHz CW-only is a voluntary plan; it is §97.305(a)+(c) table arithmetic (CW everywhere, everything else from 50.1 up). Conversely the *frequencies within* those segments (calling frequencies etc.) are voluntary.
