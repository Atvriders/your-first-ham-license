## 1. Exam logistics

- **FACT 1:** Every US amateur operator license examination must be administered by a team of at least 3 volunteer examiners (VEs) at a session coordinated by a Volunteer Examiner Coordinator (VEC). — Source: 47 CFR §97.509(a), eCFR, https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-47/part-97/section-97.509
- **FACT 2:** Each administering VE must be accredited by the coordinating VEC, be at least 18 years old, hold an Amateur Extra, Advanced, or General class license for Technician exams (Extra/Advanced for General; Extra only for Extra), and never have had an amateur license revoked or suspended. — Source: 47 CFR §97.509(b), eCFR, https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-47/part-97/section-97.509
- **FACT 3:** VEs must grade each examination element immediately upon completion (for remotely administered exams, at the earliest practical opportunity), and the VEs alone determine correctness. — Source: 47 CFR §97.509(h), eCFR, https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-47/part-97/section-97.509
- **FACT 4:** When an examinee passes, 3 VEs certify that the examinee is qualified for the license grant, and the VEs must issue a Certificate of Successful Completion of Examination (CSCE). — Source: 47 CFR §97.509(i) and (l), eCFR, https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-47/part-97/section-97.509
- **FACT 5:** After a successful exam the VEs submit the application document to the coordinating VEC; the VEC screens the information, resolves discrepancies, and forwards all required data to the FCC electronically. — Source: FCC, Volunteer Examiner Coordinators, https://www.fcc.gov/wireless/bureau-divisions/mobility-division/amateur-radio-service/volunteer-examiner-coordinators
- **FACT 6:** When an examinee fails an element, the VEs must return the application document to the examinee and inform the examinee of the grade. — Source: 47 CFR §97.509(j), eCFR, https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-47/part-97/section-97.509
- **FACT 7:** No compromised examination may be administered, and the same question set may never be re-administered to the same examinee. — Source: 47 CFR §97.509(f), eCFR, https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-47/part-97/section-97.509
- **FACT 8:** ARRL VEC retest policy: a failed element may be retaken at the same session only if (1) the team has a different version of that element the applicant has not taken, (2) the team has the time, resources, and willingness, and (3) the applicant pays an additional test fee. — Source: ARRL Volunteer Examiner Manual, "Retesting" section, http://www.arrl.org/files/file/VEs/VE%20Manual%20Web%20Final%202022.pdf
- **FACT 9:** The 2026 ARRL VEC exam session fee is $15.00, and that one fee pays for one attempt at each of the three exam elements. — Source: ARRL VEC Exam Fees ("THE 2026 ARRL VEC EXAM FEE IS $15.00"), http://www.arrl.org/arrl-vec-exam-fees
- **FACT 10:** Candidates younger than 18 pay a reduced $5 ARRL VEC exam session fee, and ARRL's Youth Licensing Grant Program reimburses the one-time $35 FCC application fee for new-license candidates under 18 who test under the ARRL VEC program. — Source: ARRL, What to Bring to an Exam Session, http://www.arrl.org/what-to-bring-to-an-exam-session
- **FACT 11:** Candidates must present one legal photo ID (state driver's license, government passport, military/law-enforcement photo ID, student school photo ID, or state photo ID) or, if none, two forms of non-photo ID (e.g., birth certificate with seal, Social Security card). — Source: ARRL, What to Bring to an Exam Session, http://www.arrl.org/what-to-bring-to-an-exam-session
- **FACT 12:** Every applicant must answer the Basic Qualification Question (felony conviction status) on the application form; a "YES" answer triggers additional FCC procedures. — Source: ARRL, What to Bring to an Exam Session, http://www.arrl.org/what-to-bring-to-an-exam-session
- **FACT 13:** The NCVEC Quick-Form 605 is the standard exam-session application: the applicant completes Section 1 (email address and FRN are mandatory), the three administering VEs print, sign, and date Section 2, and the form is sent to the coordinating VEC — never directly to the FCC. — Source: ARRL, NCVEC 605 Instructions, http://www.arrl.org/605-instructions
- **FACT 14:** The current NCVEC Form 605 is the 2022 edition, published as a PDF by NCVEC (HTTP 200, application/pdf, verified 2026-07-23). — Source: NCVEC, https://www.ncvec.org/downloads/NCVEC_Form_605_2022.pdf
- **FACT 15:** Amateur exams are offered both in person and as remote video-supervised online sessions; ARRL's exam search page ("Find an In-Person or Remote Video Online Exam") covers both and also lists sessions sponsored by non-ARRL VEC organizations. — Source: ARRL, Find an Amateur Radio License Exam Session, http://www.arrl.org/find-an-amateur-radio-license-exam-session
- **FACT 16:** VECs and VEs may only be reimbursed for out-of-pocket expenses incurred in preparing, processing, administering, or coordinating an examination (this is the legal basis for exam session fees). — Source: 47 CFR §97.527 as quoted at ARRL VEC Exam Fees, http://www.arrl.org/arrl-vec-exam-fees
- **FACT 17:** A typical ARRL VEC session tests about ten people and lasts about 3.5 hours. — Source: ARRL Volunteer Examiner Manual ("The average ARRL/VEC session tests ten people and lasts about 3-1/2 hours"), http://www.arrl.org/files/file/VEs/VE%20Manual%20Web%20Final%202022.pdf
- **FACT 64:** Typical session flow (as one VEC describes it): registration — complete the form(s), show photo ID, hand over any element-credit documents (CSCE, expired license); pre-exam instructions (calculators, rules, retest policy); the monitored exam itself; immediate grading with the result given; and a CSCE if you passed. — Source: Laurel VEC FAQ, "What will the exam session be like?", https://larc-vec.org/faq.php
- **FACT 65:** Nothing in FCC rules entitles a failed candidate to an immediate retest — "The FCC does not require VEs to let you retake a failed exam"; offering a second (different-version) exam at the same session is entirely the VE team's decision. — Source: Laurel VEC FAQ, "If I fail my exam can I retest right away?", https://larc-vec.org/faq.php

## 2. FRN, ULS, and the $35 application fee

- **FACT 18:** An FRN (FCC Registration Number) is a 10-digit number assigned to a business or individual registering with the FCC, used to identify all of the registrant's business dealings with the FCC. — Source: FCC CORES FAQ, https://apps.fcc.gov/cores/html/know.html
- **FACT 19:** FRNs are obtained by registering in CORES (COmmission REgistration System) with an FCC username account, and an FRN registered online is available immediately. — Source: FCC CORES FAQ, https://apps.fcc.gov/cores/html/know.html
- **FACT 20:** Exam candidates are required to register in CORES and have their FRN before exam day; the SSN is given to the FCC inside CORES registration, not on the exam form — the Form 605's mandatory identifier fields are the FRN and an email address. — Source: ARRL, Find an Amateur Radio License Exam Session (FRN required before exam day), http://www.arrl.org/find-an-amateur-radio-license-exam-session; ARRL, What to Bring to an Exam Session (SSN registered in CORES), http://www.arrl.org/what-to-bring-to-an-exam-session; ARRL, NCVEC 605 Instructions (mandatory fields), http://www.arrl.org/605-instructions
- **FACT 21:** A valid email address is mandatory on the license application form because the FCC sends all correspondence — including the official copy of the license — by email. — Source: ARRL, What to Bring to an Exam Session, http://www.arrl.org/what-to-bring-to-an-exam-session
- **FACT 22:** The pool itself tests the email requirement: T1C04 teaches that revocation of the station license or suspension of the operator license may follow if the FCC is unable to reach you by email. — Source: `canon/pool-technician.json`, question T1C04 (NCVEC 2026–2030 pool)
- **FACT 23:** The Universal Licensing System (ULS) is the FCC's licensing database, and your operating authority begins only when your license grant appears in it — not when you pass the exam. — Source: FCC, Amateur Radio Service, https://www.fcc.gov/wireless/bureau-divisions/mobility-division/amateur-radio-service (same rule tested by pool T1C10 in `canon/pool-technician.json`)
- **FACT 24:** The FCC charges a $35 application fee, effective April 19, 2022, on applications for a new license, a renewal, a rule waiver, or a modification requesting a new vanity call sign — per application. — Source: ARRL, FCC Application Fee, http://www.arrl.org/fcc-application-fee (FCC's fee-schedule page logs the underlying March 23, 2022 Public Notice "New Wireless Application Fee Rates – Effective April 19, 2022": https://www.fcc.gov/licensing-databases/fees/application-processing-fees)
- **FACT 25:** License upgrades to a higher operator class, administrative updates (name, mailing or email address), requests for a sequentially issued call sign, and license cancellations are exempt from the FCC application fee. — Source: ARRL, FCC Application Fee, http://www.arrl.org/fcc-application-fee
- **FACT 26:** VECs and VE teams must not collect the $35 fee at exam sessions; the fee is paid online directly to the FCC through the CORES payment system. — Source: ARRL, FCC Application Fee, http://www.arrl.org/fcc-application-fee
- **FACT 27:** When the FCC receives the exam application from the VEC, it emails a payment-instructions link to the candidate, who then has 10 calendar days from issuance of the application file number to pay; the candidate can also pay sooner by looking up the pending application by FRN in the FCC application search. — Source: ARRL, FCC Application Fee, http://www.arrl.org/fcc-application-fee
- **FACT 28:** If the fee is not paid within the 10-day window the FCC dismisses the application, but the candidate does not have to retest — the coordinating VEC can refile the application at any time before the CSCE expires. — Source: ARRL, FCC Application Fee, http://www.arrl.org/fcc-application-fee
- **FACT 29:** After the fee is paid and the application processed, the FCC emails a link to the official electronic license and provides no paper license documents; the download link in that email is valid for 30 days. — Source: ARRL, NCVEC 605 Instructions, http://www.arrl.org/605-instructions (email/no-paper); ARRL, FCC Application Fee, http://www.arrl.org/fcc-application-fee (30-day link validity)
- **FACT 30:** An amateur service license is normally granted for a 10-year term. — Source: 47 CFR §97.25, eCFR, https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-47/part-97/section-97.25
- **FACT 31:** A renewal may be filed no more than 90 days before the license expiration date, and renewals filed since April 19, 2022 carry the $35 application fee. — Source: FCC, Common Amateur Filing Tasks, https://www.fcc.gov/wireless/support/knowledge-base/common-amateur-filing-tasks (rule basis: 47 CFR §1.949(a) via §97.21(a)(3), pinned in `canon/research/r1-part97.md`)
- **FACT 32:** A person whose license has expired may still apply for renewal during a 2-year filing grace period, but no operating privileges exist unless and until the renewal is granted. — Source: 47 CFR §97.21(b), eCFR, https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-47/part-97/section-97.21
- **FACT 66:** Laurel VEC states the typical post-exam grant timing plainly: "You can start operating as soon as your license grant is shown in the FCC's ULS license database. This normally occurs the next business day after you pay the application fee." — Source: Laurel VEC FAQ, "How soon after I pass my exam can I start operating an amateur radio station?", https://larc-vec.org/faq.php
- **FACT 69:** An applicant can check application status in ULS on the next business day after a successful submission. — Source: FCC, Common Amateur Filing Tasks, https://www.fcc.gov/wireless/support/knowledge-base/common-amateur-filing-tasks
- **FACT 70:** The FRN requirement is longstanding and universal: "Effective Dec. 3, 2001, all applications and remittance must use an FRN." — Source: FCC CORES FAQ, https://apps.fcc.gov/cores/html/know.html

## 3. License classes and exam elements

- **FACT 33:** The FCC currently issues new amateur operator licenses in only three classes — Technician, General, and Amateur Extra. — Source: `canon/pool-technician.json`, question T1C01 as revised by the 2026-02-19 NCVEC errata, https://ncvec.org/index.php/2026-2030-technician-question-pool
- **FACT 34:** Five operator classes exist in the rules (Novice, Technician, General, Advanced, Amateur Extra), but Novice and Advanced are legacy classes no longer issued to new applicants. — Source: 47 CFR §97.9(a), eCFR, https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-47/part-97/section-97.9
- **FACT 35:** The Technician class license requires passing Element 2 only; General requires Elements 2 and 3; Amateur Extra requires Elements 2, 3, and 4. — Source: 47 CFR §97.501, eCFR, https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-47/part-97/section-97.501
- **FACT 36:** Element 2 (Technician) is 35 questions with a minimum passing score of 26; Element 3 (General) is 35 questions/26 to pass; Element 4 (Amateur Extra) is 50 questions/37 to pass. — Source: 47 CFR §97.503, eCFR, https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-47/part-97/section-97.503
- **FACT 37:** The 2026–2030 Element 2 exam is built as 35 questions drawn one per group from the pool's 35 groups, out of a 409-question pool. — Source: `canon/pool-technician.json` (verified counts: 409 questions, 35 groups)
- **FACT 38:** Administering VEs must give examination credit for each element a CSCE shows the examinee passed within the previous 365 days. — Source: 47 CFR §97.505(b), eCFR, https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-47/part-97/section-97.505
- **FACT 39:** Each CSCE conveying element credit is individually valid for 365 days from its issue date, and no subsequently issued CSCE renews another CSCE's validity period. — Source: ARRL Volunteer Examiner Manual, CSCE section, http://www.arrl.org/files/file/VEs/VE%20Manual%20Web%20Final%202022.pdf
- **FACT 40:** A licensee who passes a higher-class exam and properly submits Form 605 to the VEs may exercise the new class privileges immediately, until final disposition of the application or 365 days after passing, whichever comes first. — Source: 47 CFR §97.9(b), eCFR, https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-47/part-97/section-97.9
- **FACT 41:** Most new amateur operators start at Technician class and may then advance to General or Amateur Extra, with exam credit given for classes already held so passed elements need not be repeated. — Source: FCC, Amateur Radio Service, https://www.fcc.gov/wireless/bureau-divisions/mobility-division/amateur-radio-service
- **FACT 42:** The Technician license grants access to all amateur frequencies above 30 MHz plus limited HF privileges; General adds privileges on all bands and modes; Amateur Extra conveys all available US amateur privileges. — Source: ARRL, Getting Licensed, http://www.arrl.org/getting-licensed
- **FACT 67:** No Morse code exam exists for any US license class — the FCC dropped the Morse code requirement effective February 23, 2007. — Source: Laurel VEC FAQ, "Do I have to pass a Morse code exam?", https://larc-vec.org/faq.php
- **FACT 72:** There is no age limit for a US amateur license ("qualified persons of any age"), and anyone may hold one except a representative of a foreign government. — Source: FCC, Amateur Radio Service, https://www.fcc.gov/wireless/bureau-divisions/mobility-division/amateur-radio-service; ARRL, Getting Licensed, http://www.arrl.org/getting-licensed
- **FACT 73:** Operating an amateur station requires an FCC license grant, and before receiving that grant you must pass an examination administered by a team of volunteer examiners. — Source: FCC, Amateur Radio Service, https://www.fcc.gov/wireless/bureau-divisions/mobility-division/amateur-radio-service

## 4. Call signs

- **FACT 43:** Each new station is assigned a call sign by the sequential call sign system from the regional-group list for the licensee's operator class and mailing address, and the station keeps the same call sign upon renewal or modification unless the licensee applies for a change. — Source: FCC, Amateur Call Sign Systems, https://www.fcc.gov/wireless/bureau-divisions/mobility-division/amateur-radio-service/amateur-call-sign-systems
- **FACT 44:** A US amateur call sign consists of a prefix of one letter (K, N, or W) or two letters (AA–AL, KA–KZ, NA–NZ, WA–WZ), a numeral 0–9 indicating the geographic region, and a suffix of one to three letters. — Source: FCC, Amateur Call Sign Systems, https://www.fcc.gov/wireless/bureau-divisions/mobility-division/amateur-radio-service/amateur-call-sign-systems
- **FACT 45:** The sequential system has four groups: Group A for Amateur Extra (1×2, 2×1, and A-first-prefix 2×2 formats), Group B for Advanced (2×2 with K/N/W-first prefix), Group C for General/Technician (1×3), and Group D for Novice, club, and military recreation stations (2×3); when a region's group list is exhausted, assignment moves to the next lower group. — Source: FCC, Amateur Call Sign Systems, https://www.fcc.gov/wireless/bureau-divisions/mobility-division/amateur-radio-service/amateur-call-sign-systems
- **FACT 46:** The numeral is the call district: regions 1–10 cover the contiguous states (e.g., 1 = New England, 6 = California, 0 = the tenth region), with Alaska as region 11, the Caribbean insular areas as region 12, and Hawaii/Pacific insular areas as region 13. — Source: FCC, Amateur Call Sign Systems, https://www.fcc.gov/wireless/bureau-divisions/mobility-division/amateur-radio-service/amateur-call-sign-systems
- **FACT 47:** The pool tests call-sign format knowledge with T1C05 — a valid Group D call sign format for a Technician is the 2×3 format (correct answer: KF1XXX) — and tests vanity eligibility with T1C02 (any licensed amateur may select a vanity call sign). — Source: `canon/pool-technician.json`, questions T1C05 and T1C02
- **FACT 48:** Vanity call sign requests are filed through ULS with up to 25 call signs listed in order of preference, each given as an exact prefix, numeral, and suffix, and the first assignable eligible call sign is granted while the old call sign is vacated. — Source: FCC, Amateur Call Sign Systems, https://www.fcc.gov/wireless/bureau-divisions/mobility-division/amateur-radio-service/amateur-call-sign-systems
- **FACT 49:** A vanity applicant may only receive a call sign from a group corresponding to the applicant's operator class or lower — a Technician may receive only Group C or D formats, and requesting a Group A or B call sign gets the application dismissed. — Source: FCC, Amateur Call Sign Systems, https://www.fcc.gov/wireless/bureau-divisions/mobility-division/amateur-radio-service/amateur-call-sign-systems (rule text: 47 CFR §97.19(d), https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-47/part-97/section-97.19)
- **FACT 50:** Military recreation stations are not eligible for vanity call signs. — Source: 47 CFR §97.19(a), eCFR, https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-47/part-97/section-97.19
- **FACT 51:** A call sign normally becomes assignable again 2 years after license expiration, surrender, cancellation, or the grantee's death (with limited exceptions for close relatives and former holders). — Source: FCC, Amateur Call Sign Systems, https://www.fcc.gov/wireless/bureau-divisions/mobility-division/amateur-radio-service/amateur-call-sign-systems (rule text: 47 CFR §97.19(c))
- **FACT 52:** Club station and military recreation station license applications and changes are handled through Club Station Call Sign Administrators (CSCSA) using forms such as the ARRL VEC Form 605-C — not via the NCVEC Form 605 and not directly with the FCC. — Source: ARRL, NCVEC 605 Instructions, http://www.arrl.org/605-instructions (rule text: 47 CFR §97.21(a)(1))
- **FACT 53:** The special event call sign system offers a block of 750 "1×1" call signs (single K/N/W letter, single digit, single letter — e.g., K1A) coordinated by FCC-certified special event coordinators, and a station using one must still transmit its assigned call sign at least once per hour. — Source: FCC, Special Event Call Signs, https://www.fcc.gov/wireless/bureau-divisions/mobility-division/amateur-radio-service/special-event-call-signs
- **FACT 71:** Some call signs are never assigned: prefixes AM–AZ (ITU-assigned to other countries), suffixes SOS or QRA–QUZ, any 2×3 with X as the first suffix letter, reserved government/FEMA formats, and the 1×1 formats (reserved for the special event system). — Source: FCC, Amateur Call Sign Systems, "Call Sign Availability", https://www.fcc.gov/wireless/bureau-divisions/mobility-division/amateur-radio-service/amateur-call-sign-systems

## 5. Finding a session and study resources

- **FACT 54:** ARRL's license-exam search locates in-person sessions by ZIP code and links to a list of VE teams that offer online exam sessions; it is updated daily and includes non-ARRL VEC sessions (the listing's "VEC" line identifies the sponsor). — Source: ARRL, Find an Amateur Radio License Exam Session, http://www.arrl.org/find-an-amateur-radio-license-exam-session
- **FACT 55:** Laurel VEC (Laurel Amateur Radio Club VEC) has administered free amateur radio license exams since 1984, and states that the VEC and its VEs do not charge any fees for any licensing-related services. — Source: Laurel VEC FAQ, https://larc-vec.org/faq.php
- **FACT 56:** Laurel VEC's legacy domain laurelvec.com now redirects (HTTP 307) to https://larc-vec.org/ (verified 2026-07-23). — Source: curl header check, https://www.laurelvec.com/
- **FACT 57:** HamStudy.org offers free stats-driven flash cards, question lists, explanations, and practice tests built on the published question pools, and its session search page ("Find an Exam Session") lists both in-person and remote exam sessions run by many VE teams. — Source: HamStudy.org, https://hamstudy.org/ (session listings: https://hamstudy.org/sessions)
- **FACT 58:** The FCC publishes the roster of authorized VECs and notes that every VEC is authorized to coordinate examination sessions at any location an examination can be administered. — Source: FCC, Volunteer Examiner Coordinators, https://www.fcc.gov/wireless/bureau-divisions/mobility-division/amateur-radio-service/volunteer-examiner-coordinators
- **FACT 68:** Laurel VEC itself runs in-person exams only — "All examinees must be physically in the presence of three administering VEs at all times" — so remote testing must be found with other VECs/teams. — Source: Laurel VEC FAQ, "Can I take an examination online at home?", https://larc-vec.org/faq.php

## 6. Pool currency

- **FACT 59:** FCC rules require all VECs to cooperate in maintaining one question pool per written exam element; each pool must contain at least 10 times the number of questions on a single exam and must be published and available to the public before use. — Source: 47 CFR §97.523, eCFR, https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-47/part-97/section-97.523
- **FACT 60:** Every examination question set must utilize questions taken from the applicable question pool. — Source: 47 CFR §97.507(b), eCFR, https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-47/part-97/section-97.507
- **FACT 61:** The NCVEC Question Pool Committee released the 2026–2030 Technician Class (Element 2) question pool into the public domain on December 18, 2025. — Source: NCVEC, 2026–2030 Technician Question Pool ("The NCVEC Question Pool Committee (QPC) hereby releases the 2026-2030 Technician Class (Element 2) Question Pool into the public domain"), https://ncvec.org/index.php/2026-2030-technician-question-pool
- **FACT 62:** The 2026–2030 Technician pool is effective for exams from 2026-07-01 through 2030-06-30, and question pools are reviewed and updated on a four-year rotation (General: effective 2023-07-01, valid to 2027-06-30; Extra: effective 2024-07-01, valid to 2028-06-30; no pools updated or released in 2025 or 2029). — Source: ARRL, Question Pools, http://www.arrl.org/question-pools
- **FACT 63:** An errata issued February 19, 2026 modified 4 questions in the originally released 2026–2030 Technician pool (T1C01, T5A05, T7A09, T0A10), and the current published pool documents already incorporate those changes. — Source: NCVEC, 2026–2030 Technician Question Pool, https://ncvec.org/index.php/2026-2030-technician-question-pool (incorporation verified in `canon/ingestion-report.md` and in `canon/pool-technician.json`)

## Watch items

- **Fee amount ($35):** verified 2026-07-23 against arrl.org/fcc-application-fee, the Laurel VEC FAQ ("The FCC charges an application fee of $35"), and the FCC fee-schedule page (which lists FY 2025 fee documents with no amateur change). Application fees are set by FCC order and can be adjusted in any future fiscal-year fee order — re-verify before each reprint.
- **ARRL VEC exam fee ($15 / $5 youth):** explicitly a calendar-2026 figure ("THE 2026 ARRL VEC EXAM FEE IS $15.00") — re-verify each January; other VECs set their own fees (usually $15 or less per ARRL), and Laurel VEC charges nothing.
- **Grant timeline after the exam:** the only pinned figure is Laurel VEC's FAQ — grant "normally occurs the next business day after you pay the application fee" (Fact 66) — plus the FCC's up-to-10-day payment window. No official FCC-wide guarantee exists; VEC processing time varies. Book should say "typically a few days to about two weeks" at most, and stress the hard rule: no transmitting until the grant appears in ULS (Fact 23). Do not print a specific day count as a promise.
- **10-day payment window and 30-day license-link validity:** sourced from ARRL's fee page (not an FCC document); the FCC sends the emails, so wording in the book should attribute the mechanics to the FCC process as described by ARRL VEC.
- UNVERIFIED: that CORES/FRN registration is explicitly "free." No payment step exists in the registration flow and no source mentions any charge, but no primary sentence states "free of charge" (re-checked FCC CORES FAQ, ARRL pages, ARRL VE Manual on 2026-07-23). Safe book wording: "register online in CORES — there's no fee and no exam requirement."
- **NCVEC Form 605 edition:** the live form is the 2022 edition (verified 2026-07-23); check ncvec.org for a newer revision before publication, since the form's mandatory fields (FRN, email, felony question) drive chapter-10 instructions.
- **Laurel VEC domain:** laurelvec.com 307-redirects to larc-vec.org (verified 2026-07-23); cite larc-vec.org in the book.
- **Remote exams:** availability depends entirely on the individual VE team; there is no single universal remote-exam program, and some VECs (e.g., Laurel, Fact 68) do not offer remote testing at all — point readers at ARRL's online-session list and hamstudy.org/sessions rather than promising remote testing.
- **eCFR "current" URLs** always render the latest rule text; all rule quotes above were captured 2026-07-23. If Part 97 is amended, re-pull the sections cited.
